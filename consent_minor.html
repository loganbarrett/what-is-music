Permission Form for Parents and Guardians, and a Note for Young People: Is It Music?

For the parent or guardian. Your child is invited to take part in a research study run by the Auditory Perception & Action Lab, Department of Research, Niagara Falls Memorial Medical Center. The person in charge is Logan Barrett. Please read this page, and ask your child to read the part written for them.

What the study is about

People disagree about what counts as music. We want to understand where people draw the line between music and other sounds, and how that line differs between people.

What your child will do

  • Answer a few optional background questions (their gender, how much music training they have had, how often they make music, how much they listen to music, and what they are listening on). We do not ask for a name, email, school or home location.
  • Listen to very short sounds (about 5 seconds each): music of many kinds, spoken recordings, chanting, animal and bird sounds, bells, machines and everyday sounds.
  • For each sound, decide whether it is music or not music by swiping or pressing a button. There are no right or wrong answers. They answer as many as they like and can stop at any time.

It takes as long as they choose; a few minutes is typical. Headphones are recommended.

Risks and benefits

There is no risk beyond that of everyday life. Some sounds may be loud or unpleasant (for example a siren or a baby crying), and some music or spoken recordings come from public archives and may contain strong language or mature themes. The clips are not screened for content, so a parent or guardian may wish to listen along. The benefit to your child is seeing where they draw the line between music and other sounds. There is no payment.

What information is collected, and how it is kept

We collect your child's answer (music or not music) for each sound, how quickly they answered, how often a sound replayed, their answers to the optional background questions, and an anonymous ID that the page makes up. We do not collect their name or email, and we do not use their microphone or record any sound. The web server that hosts this page may keep ordinary technical logs (such as an IP address) that are not part of the research data; this page is hosted on GitHub Pages, which keeps such logs for security, and the researchers do not see them.

Data from people under 18 are saved in a separate folder from adult data and are labelled as under 18. They are analysed separately from adult data. They are kept for 10 years in a password-protected folder belonging to the lab on Google Drive and on a password-protected computer.

Taking part is voluntary

You and your child may decide not to take part. Your child can still play the game on the page without any data being sent. If you agree, your child may stop at any time by closing the page. The page shows an anonymous participant ID at the end. To ask for the data to be removed, send that ID to the contact below. Without the ID we cannot find the file.

Questions

If you have questions about this study, contact Logan Barrett (logan.barrett@nfmmc.org). If you have questions about your child's rights as a research participant, contact the Niagara Falls Memorial Medical Center IRB office at irb@nfmmc.org. The IRB study number is IRB20261103.

Permission

On the game page, a parent or guardian ticks the box and presses I agree to confirm that they have read this form, that they are the parent or legal guardian of the young person taking part, and that they give permission.

For young people: a note for you

This is a game about sounds. You will listen to some very short sounds and decide whether each one is music or not. There are no right or wrong answers. You do not have to do it, and you can stop whenever you want, even if your parent or guardian said yes. If you tick the box you are saying that you understand this and that you want to play.

You can print this page (Ctrl/Cmd + P) to keep a copy. Close this tab to return to the game.
